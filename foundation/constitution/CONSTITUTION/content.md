# ATON Constitution

Version 1.0.0

---

# Preamble

Engineering systems continue to grow in complexity.

Engineering knowledge is created, reviewed, governed and evolved over long
periods of time by many people, organizations and technologies.

The ATON Foundation exists to provide a common semantic foundation for
engineering knowledge and its evolution.

ATON is an open Engineering Operating System.

It defines engineering concepts, relationships, operations and governance
independently of engineering processes, technologies and implementations.

This Constitution defines the enduring purpose, principles and boundaries
upon which the ATON Foundation is built.

---

# Article I — Purpose

## Section 1 — Mission

The mission of the ATON Foundation is to establish a common semantic
foundation for engineering.

The Foundation enables engineering knowledge to be created, organized,
related, governed, evolved and exchanged consistently across organizations,
domains and technologies.

---

## Section 2 — Objectives

The Foundation provides:

- a common engineering language
- a shared semantic model
- implementation-independent engineering concepts
- explicit engineering relationships
- traceable engineering evolution
- a stable basis for engineering interoperability

---

## Section 3 — Independence

The Foundation remains independent of:

- engineering domains
- engineering processes
- implementation technologies
- persistence mechanisms
- user interfaces
- software implementations

The Foundation defines semantics rather than implementations or workflows.

---

# Article II — Scope

## Section 1 — The Foundation

The Foundation defines the semantic architecture of the Engineering
Operating System.

It establishes the concepts, relationships, operations and constraints
required to model engineering knowledge and its evolution.

The Foundation does not prescribe how engineering is performed.

---

## Section 2 — Responsibilities

The Foundation defines:

- engineering concepts
- engineering relationships
- engineering operations
- engineering constraints
- semantic rules
- engineering identity
- engineering traceability

---

## Section 3 — Boundaries

The Foundation does not define:

- engineering processes
- organizational structures
- project workflows
- implementation technologies
- storage mechanisms
- communication protocols
- presentation formats
- user interfaces

These concerns belong to higher architectural layers.

---

## Section 4 — Extensibility

The Foundation is intended to be extended.

Extensions preserve semantic consistency and interoperability.

Extensions may introduce:

- additional concepts
- domain libraries
- profiles
- capabilities
- applications

---

# Article III — Principles

The Foundation is governed by the following principles.

## Principle 1 — Semantic Foundation

Engineering is described through well-defined engineering concepts and
their semantics.

Semantics remain independent of representation and implementation.

---

## Principle 2 — Explicit Relationships

Engineering knowledge is connected through explicit relationships.

Relationships express engineering meaning rather than implementation
structure.

---

## Principle 3 — Engineering Evolution

Engineering knowledge evolves throughout its lifecycle.

The Foundation supports controlled and traceable engineering evolution.

---

## Principle 4 — Task-Driven Engineering

Engineering work is planned and governed through engineering tasks.

Tasks coordinate engineering work without prescribing engineering
processes.

---

## Principle 5 — Traceability

Engineering knowledge and engineering work remain traceable throughout
their lifecycle.

---

## Principle 6 — Process Independence

The Foundation defines engineering semantics.

Engineering processes are defined by configurable process profiles.

---

## Principle 7 — Technology Independence

Engineering semantics remain independent of implementation technologies.

---

## Principle 8 — Extensibility

The Foundation is designed for long-term extensibility.

---

## Principle 9 — Open Governance

The evolution of the Foundation is open, transparent and traceable.

---

## Principle 10 — Long-Term Stability

Long-term semantic stability takes precedence over short-term
implementation convenience.

---

# Article IV — Architecture

## Section 1 — Architectural Foundation

The Foundation establishes the semantic architecture of the Engineering
Operating System.

It provides the stable basis upon which engineering knowledge,
engineering work and engineering governance are built.

---

## Section 2 — Layered Architecture

The Foundation defines semantics.

Profiles define policies and engineering processes.

Capabilities provide engineering services.

Applications provide user interaction.

Implementations realize these concepts using specific technologies.

---

## Section 3 — Separation of Concerns

Each architectural layer has a distinct responsibility.

This separation preserves clarity, modularity and long-term
maintainability.

---

## Section 4 — Engineering Kernel

The Engineering Kernel forms the semantic core of the Foundation.

It consists of engineering concepts, relationships, operations and
constraints.

The Engineering Kernel remains independent of engineering domains,
technologies and implementations.

---

# Article V — Governance

## Section 1 — Evolution

The Foundation is intended to evolve while preserving the principles
defined by this Constitution.

---

## Section 2 — Engineering Governance

The evolution of the Foundation follows a transparent, traceable and
collaborative engineering process.

Architecture Decisions document architectural rationale.

Specifications define normative behavior.

Engineering Tasks coordinate engineering work.

---

## Section 3 — Stewardship

The Foundation is maintained on behalf of its community.

Engineering decisions prioritize semantic consistency, interoperability
and long-term stability.

---

## Section 4 — Transparency

Architectural decisions remain understandable, documented and publicly
accessible.

---

# Article VI — Evolution and Amendment

## Section 1 — Evolution

The Foundation evolves continuously through engineering.

---

## Section 2 — Amendments

The Constitution may evolve while preserving the identity and purpose of
the Foundation.

Changes are supported by explicit architectural rationale and transparent
governance.

---

## Section 3 — Continuity

The Constitution is the highest architectural document of the ATON
Foundation.

Architecture Decisions, Specifications, Profiles, Capabilities,
Applications and Implementations remain consistent with the principles
defined by this Constitution.
