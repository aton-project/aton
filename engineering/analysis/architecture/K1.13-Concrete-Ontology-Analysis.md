# K1.13 — Concrete Ontology Analysis

Status: Draft
Analysis Type: Engineering Analysis
Normative Status: Non-normative

This is the original, unreviewed agent analysis for TASK-0002. It records evidence and candidate clarifications, not architecture acceptance or implementation requirements.

## 1. Executive Summary

ATON supports K1 substantially but does not yet define a consistent, complete concrete ontology across the three levels. Accepted ADR-0004 distinguishes identifiable engineering knowledge from its canonical Artifacts and rejects file-centric organization. The Constitution separates semantics from representation. Draft RFC-0010 explicitly defines a logical Artifact with identity independent of its physical representation; Proposed RFC-0030 explicitly places directories and the three component files on the physical side.

The concrete ontology remains entirely Draft according to its metadata. Many definitions identify semantic engineering meaning without specifying its relationship to a logical Artifact. Artifact, ADR, RFC, Review, Finding, GlossaryEntry and Constitution contain terminology requiring particular care. Metadata and relation ownership are inconsistent across sources. Thing supplies a general category but no complete taxonomy. Physical Representation exists in RFC prose, not as a concrete ontology concept in the inspected directory.

The central answer is therefore **partially supported, with unresolved relationships and some conflicting definitions**. The sources do not justify a strict containment hierarchy, Entity/Artifact disjointness, mandatory one-to-one mappings, or file-based identity. Several detailed conclusions depend on Draft RFCs or Proposed ADRs and are not accepted architectural obligations.

## 5. K1 Semantic Baseline

For this task, Entity means a semantically identifiable unit of engineering knowledge; Artifact means a logical engineering representation or expression of knowledge; Physical Representation means a concrete technical manifestation or serialization of an Artifact.

These are distinctions in meaning and identity roles. They do not decide inheritance, containment, disjointness, cardinality or whether a managed logical Artifact can also be an identifiable engineering knowledge object. A decision and its documentation can have distinct identities even if a future taxonomy permits both to participate in Entity semantics.

Accepted ADR-0004's Decision supports Entity representation by Artifacts and independent Artifact identification/versioning. Draft RFC-0010, Engineering Artifact and Multiple Physical Representations, explicitly supplies the three-level separation. Proposed ADR-0013 and ONT-Artifact instead use persisted/addressable and physical/addressable language: compatible intentions, incomplete logical/physical separation.

Proposed RFC-0015 additionally permits technical representations of other canonical knowledge. This broader source model is recorded; K1 is not used to impose mandatory Artifact mediation on every metadata value, relation or View. Canonical domain objects used by the kernel are an architectural translation boundary, not an automatic fourth ontology superclass.

## 6. Individual Ontology Concept Analysis

### Common answers and limitations applicable to every concept

The following common findings apply to each entry below and answer recurring parts of the 18-question examination without inventing 23 independent identity policies.

1. **Concept versus instance:** each ONT definition has an explicit `id: ONT-<name>`, `entityType: ontology`, `status: Draft`. This is evidence of concept identity, not proof that every instance has an independent UUID or even an independent managed identity. The ONT metadata generally omits `ontologyType`; classification of definition objects remains incomplete relative to accepted ADR-0008.
2. **Physical evidence:** every concept definition is physically represented by `foundation/specifications/ontology/<name>/content.md` and `metadata.yaml`. All except AX and Constitution also have `relations.yaml`. These files represent definitions of categories, not instances of all categories. No requirement, component, test or decision instance is inferred solely from the corresponding ontology directory.
3. **Generic representation permissions:** Draft RFC-0001 and RFC-0010 permit multiple Artifacts for an Entity and one Artifact describing multiple Entities. RFC-0010 permits several physical representations for a logical Artifact. These are supported general possibilities, not automatically specified per-concept minima, maxima or required mappings.
4. **Identity stability:** ENTITY-0001, RFC-0002 and RFC-0010 protect semantic/logical identity against relocation and format changes. They provide principles; most concrete ONT definitions do not provide sufficient instance identity/continuity criteria. No UUID mandate follows. Paths, filenames and Git locations are physical access/provenance, not semantic identities.
5. **Relation evidence:** most ONT definition sidecars contain only empty `references`; ONT-Metadata references RFC-0026, RFC-0027 and RFC-0029 and has other empty lists. AX and Constitution have no relation sidecar. These are relations of the definition artifacts, not a full instance endpoint contract. The instance predicate vocabulary in Draft RFC-0027 is separately reported below; only four predicates have inspected concrete definition directories.
6. **Evolution:** Proposed ADR-0012 and Draft RFC-0012 distinguish Engineering Version, Artifact Revision and Git revision. The concrete ONT definitions have no complete per-instance revision/baseline policies. Changes to a definition, a represented instance, its documentation and a file may affect different histories.

Each entry states its own meaning, likely identity kind, represented subject, artifact role, physical realization, endpoint issues and necessary clarification. “Supported interpretation” is deliberately weaker than an accepted taxonomy claim.

### 6.1. Thing

**Definition and role (documented):** Most general conceptual category: anything meaningfully identifiable in the knowledge model. Primarily semantic, with unspecified scope. Source: ONT-Thing, Definition or opening definition; all are Draft.

**Identity and representation:** General conceptual identity is suggested by identifiability; independent instance identity rules are unresolved. Neither an Entity superclass nor an Artifact superclass is actually established. A Thing may be described by an Artifact under a future applicable model, but ONT-Thing itself gives no representation relationship. The definition's physical files are identified in the common findings above; an instance's particular file layout is not implied by that definition layout. Multiple Artifacts/physical representations are generic supported possibilities where the corresponding logical role applies, not a new concept-specific mandate.

**Relations and stability:** No Thing-specific allowed pair occurs in RFC-0027. Pattern-based references may match a valid Thing type under RFC-0029. Concept ID is explicit; instance UUID/path/filename/Git identity requirements are not specified locally. Generic Entity/Artifact independence supports relocation continuity, but domain-specific identity and version boundaries remain incomplete. No new Engineering Version or Artifact Revision is inferred from a Git commit or a metadata status string alone.

**K1 assessment and candidate clarification:** Clarify scope and its relationship to Entity, Artifact and technical objects; do not infer inheritance from “most general.”

## 9. Taxonomy Analysis

Documented vocabulary is not a coherent complete taxonomy. ONT-Thing calls itself the most general category; ONT-Entity calls every represented engineering object an Entity; RFC-0027 classifies artifacts as Concept instances and gives specialization examples ADR -> Decision and RFC -> Artifact. Neither those examples nor directory proximity establishes a complete accepted set of specialization edges.

Draft RFC-0020 explicitly permits entity, artifact, metadata, predicate, process and other concept kinds. Draft RFC-0021 and RFC-0022 distinguish Views/Collections from Entity and Artifact, challenging a literal universal Entity rule. ONT-Predicate's Artifact wording also differs from RFC-0027's warning about ordinary Artifact classification. These are definition/taxonomy conflicts within Draft material, not proof that the Constitution requires one particular hierarchy.

Missing contracts are the scope of Thing, Entity universality/overlap, logical Artifact category membership, ontology concept versus definition Artifact, View/Collection identity roles, and technical representation scope. There is no PhysicalRepresentation ontology directory in the inventory, although RFC-0010/0015 define it in prose. Adding a taxonomy node or inheritance edge is not a direct K1 consequence; clarifying the distinction is.

| Identity/field | Repository evidence | Assessment |

ENTITY-0001 explicitly protects Entity identity from filename, directory, format and rendered documentation. RFC-0010 separately protects Artifact identity from representation changes. ONT-Artifact and TERM-Artifact mostly protect the represented Entity and do not equally spell out same-Artifact continuity. The distinction matters for moving one record versus replacing it with a different record of the same subject.

Concrete F-001 is not globally unambiguous by string alone: it occurs within review contexts. REVIEW-0001's contains entries use local F-* strings; finding metadata/relations do not by themselves define a globally stable context resolution contract. Proposed ADR-0015 permits context-local authoring IDs only with unambiguous canonical resolution. Physical nesting is evidence of current storage context, not a settled semantic identity mechanism.

The generic model offers sufficient independence principles but insufficient concrete identity rules for split/merge, equivalent rewording, regenerated records, duplicated representations and cross-scope references. These remain decisions requiring evidence and domain judgment.

## 11. Typing Analysis

Accepted ADR-0008, Explicit Ontology Type Identity, explicitly assigns `ontologyType` to an Artifact participating in the semantic model, exactly one canonical Concept ID, with no inference from paths, names, format or artifact classification. This is direct accepted evidence of Artifact-side declaration. It is not evidence that the field automatically belongs to the physical file.

ENTITY-0001 assigns one conceptual type to Entity. Draft RFC-0020, Ontology Type, repeats artifact-side typing while Ontology and Engineering Entities discusses Entity classification. Draft RFC-0029 resolves Entity Concepts for validation but also identifies source/target artifacts in diagnostics. Thus semantic classification is supported; universal ownership across a represented Entity and its logical Artifact is unresolved.

An RFC Artifact typed ONT-RFC can describe Requirement, Interface and Component Entities; its single type cannot silently classify all of those subjects as RFC. Conversely a Requirement Entity's conceptual type cannot automatically settle whether its documenting Artifact is a requirement record or a larger specification. K1 requires distinguishing the classified objects, but does not decide field count, metadata structure or inheritance.

All ONT definitions have `entityType: ontology`, not an explicit `ontologyType`. RFC-0029 equates canonical Concept IDs with the ontology Artifact IDs. That is a documented convention, not proof that the definition Artifact and the concept it defines are semantically identical. Self-description and the typing of metamodel objects require clarification.

## 12. Relation Analysis

Accepted ADR-0006 makes relations first-class logical graph elements. Accepted ADR-0009 supplies Entity source context and ontology-defined domain/range constraints, while refusing to equate structural existence with semantic validity. ONT-Relation and TERM-Relation describe Entity -> Entity. TERM-Predicate similarly speaks of Entity endpoints.

Draft RFC-0025 instead names Engineering Knowledge objects as source/target. Draft RFC-0010, Artifact and Relations, explicitly permits Artifact -> Artifact, Artifact -> Entity, Entity -> Artifact and other ontology-permitted combinations. Proposed ADR-0014 calls the source the owning artifact or entity. These are broader models; they cannot be silently reduced to Entity-only or silently accepted over accepted ADR-0009. Whether logical Artifacts themselves participate as Entities would alter the apparent conflict, but that taxonomy answer is not established.

### Concrete predicate observations

| Predicate | Documented pairs/prose | Sidecar constraints | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| motivates | Note -> ADR; Finding -> ADR | Same exact ONT pairs | Consistent pair specification; prose says Decision while typed target is ADR, exposing record/subject interpretation |
| references | PRED-references and RFC-0027 explicitly only Artifact -> Artifact | ANY-CONCEPT -> ANY-CONCEPT | Direct Draft prose/constraint conflict; pattern is explicitly defined in Proposed RFC-0029 and is not inheritance |
| governs | RFC-0027 ADR -> Artifact/Collection | Adds Constitution -> Artifact/Collection | Catalogue incompleteness/difference; Constitution instance targets foundation, whose endpoint type is not established here |
| refines | RFC-0027 Requirement/RFC/ADR/Component/Interface to same type | Adds AX -> AX | Catalogue/sidecar difference; semantic versus expression-level refinement needs owner clarity |

No cardinality key occurs in these four inspected constraints. That does not prove a required or prohibited number of links. Draft RFC-0026/0027 and Proposed RFC-0029 explicitly avoid implicit cardinality restrictions. Declared inverse names are not proof of stored inverse occurrences or implementations.

RFC-0027 also describes specifies, implements, verifies, defines, allocates, specializes and contains. These documented Draft vocabulary entries are not all concrete predicate artifacts in the current directory. Component/Requirement/Interface/Test pairs mix semantic objects and “artifact” prose; that is not automatically invalid under an overlapping taxonomy, but it needs endpoint role definitions.

The ONT definition sidecars mostly have empty references even where their bodies cite ENTITY-0001. A prose reference and an authored graph edge are different evidence. Similarly RFC-0031 distinguishes Markdown links from canonical relations. Proposed RFC-0030 serializes dependsOn/relatedTo while Draft RFC-0028 leaves these legacy meanings unresolved; structural serializability does not decide domain meaning.

Concrete REVIEW-0001 uses reviews and contains; its Finding uses implementedBy -> ADR-0001. RFC-0027's implements pairs permit Component -> RFC/Requirement, so an inverse involving Finding/ADR is not automatically supported. This observation is a catalogue mismatch, not a runtime error verdict or automatic migration prescription. Review records also omit ontologyType in the inspected example; endpoint classification cannot be reconstructed canonically just from entityType or path.

Required clarification is semantic source ownership inside a compound Artifact, allowed object kinds, target resolution including external targets, and authority when predicate text and sidecars differ. Proposed RFC-0029 exact matching prohibits implicit specialization-based expansion; Draft RFC-0028 explicitly preserves unresolved legacy meanings and non-artifact external Entities. No relations are rewritten by this analysis.

## 13. Metadata Analysis

ONT-Metadata and ENTITY-0001 assign metadata to Entity. Accepted ADR-0005 says metadata describes Artifacts and separates logical content/metadata/relations. Proposed ADR-0010 says metadata belongs to the logical engineering concept it describes, independent of co-location. Draft RFC-0003 and RFC-0010 explicitly permit descriptions of Entity, Artifact, revision and physical representation. Draft RFC-0005 treats canonical metadata as a specific use of properties. These scopes differ; later proposals do not automatically amend ADR-0005.

Separating content, metadata and relations into logical components does not imply three independent Entities, Artifacts or UUIDs. Proposed ADR-0013 explicitly says representation components are not automatically Entities. YAML values can describe the represented Requirement, its document Artifact or technical serialization; the current one-file metadata layout does not decide which owner a particular value has.

Canonical semantic values and derived technical facts also differ. Proposed ADR-0010 requires explicit canonical information, rejects silent overrides from Git, and distinguishes engineering-event, Git-event and physical-event timestamps. It permits explicit materialization into canonical metadata. Saving a generated file or observing a filesystem mtime does not itself perform that semantic operation.

K1 exposes the need to identify the described object. The set of mandatory fields, ownership representation, authority/conflict policy and metadata version triggers remain K1-dependent decisions. Empty schema files supply no missing validation contract.

| Entity identity | RFC-0002/0012 and Proposed ADR-0012/0013 preserve identity across semantic states | Per-concept sameness criteria |
| Engineering Version | Draft RFC-0012 identifies semantic Entity state; distinct from Git | Numbering, establishment, cross-scope IDs and exact triggers |
| Logical Artifact | Accepted ADR-0004 requires independent versionability; Draft RFC-0010 preserves identity | Relationship of independent Artifact versionability to Artifact Revision |
| Artifact Revision | Proposed ADR-0012 and Draft RFC-0010 cover logical Artifact or physical representation change | Logical/physical granularity, IDs and when conversion counts |
| Physical change | RFC-0010/0015 permit changed serialization with unchanged logical identity | Technical history versus explicitly identified Artifact Revision |
| Git commit | Accepted ADR-0003 records Foundation changes; RFC-0010/0012 explicitly reject automatic Revision/Version equivalence | Explicit provenance mapping |
| Baseline | Proposed ADR-0012 and Draft RFC-0013 select reproducible knowledge state | Membership model and bindings to Entity Versions/Artifact Revisions |

One Engineering Version may involve multiple Artifact Revisions; a compound Artifact Revision may affect multiple Entities. A physical conversion need not change any represented Entity's engineering meaning. None of this decides that every format-only edit must, or must not, create an Artifact Revision. RFC-0010 leaves revision triggers and identity open.

Lifecycle is another axis. Proposed ADR-0017 assigns architectural lifecycle to the Decision; its text also uses ADR-state/version wording, leaving document-versus-subject ownership insufficiently clear. Draft RFC-0012 treats lifecycle transitions as possible semantic version changes, not new identity or a status-as-version identifier. A Finding's acceptance, a Review's completion and an ADR's acceptance do not necessarily refer to the same object or engineering event.

Collection membership history and View evaluation/version context are separately developed in Draft RFC-0022/0021. A dynamic Collection is not automatically an immutable Baseline; a View is not the selected state. Established-version/baseline immutability is proposed/draft evidence, not a new rule introduced here. Git reproducibility alone does not identify historical semantic boundaries when they were never recorded.

## 15. Direct K1 Consequences

These follow from the established distinction, without selecting further ontology policy:

- A semantic subject, its logical expression and its technical manifestation need distinguishable meanings; a path or file existence alone cannot establish their equivalence.
- A physical move/conversion alone is insufficient evidence of changed Entity or logical Artifact identity.
- A statement about a logical subject must remain distinguishable from a statement about its documentation or serialization.
- Documentation of another object does not automatically inherit that object's identity, type, lifecycle or authority.
- Physical nesting, adjacency, field position and Git boundaries alone cannot supply semantic containment, endpoint ownership or version meaning.

K1 does not logically require separate database tables, a mandatory UUID scheme, disjoint sets, inheritance edges or one Artifact per Entity. It also does not guarantee that a semantically changed document retains identity: sameness requires the applicable semantic criteria.

## 19. Candidate Clarifications

Candidate wording goals, not changes performed:

1. Clarify logical Artifact identity separately from both represented subject and technical manifestation, preserving the generic multi-subject cases in RFC-0010 unless a later decision changes them.
2. State which uses of Entity denote a universal identifiable knowledge object and which denote a represented subject; explicitly leave overlap undecided until governed.
3. Distinguish Requirement obligation/statement, Decision/ADR, term/GlossaryEntry, Review activity/record and Finding result/record in their identity and documentation relationships.
4. Clarify Collection membership and View definition/output identity without turning directories or pages into canonical organization.
5. Identify the classified object for ontologyType and the described owner for metadata and relation source context.
6. Designate authoritative predicate meaning/constraints and reconcile catalogue differences through an explicit semantic decision.
7. Clarify Engineering Version, Artifact Revision and physical change boundaries and bindings to reproducible states.

These goals do not prescribe fields, filenames, class hierarchies or implementation migration. No candidate statement here becomes normative by being committed.

## 20. Open Questions

- Is Entity a universal category, a role, or a narrower subject category? Which logical Artifacts may also have Entity identity?
- What preserves a Requirement, Decision, Finding, Collection or View identity through semantic change, and what preserves an Artifact through regeneration, split or merge?
- Can an independently managed Note/RFC/Review Artifact lack a dedicated subject Entity, and how is its own identity classified?
- Does ontologyType classify a subject, a logical Artifact or both under distinct declarations? How are ontology-definition objects typed?
- Who owns a relation when one Artifact expresses several Entities? Are Artifact endpoints canonical in their own right or via Entity participation?
- Which metadata properties describe which object and state? What establishes authority across multiple representations?
- What is the canonical scope/resolution of repeated F-* IDs, independent of repository paths?
- Which source governs predicate applicability when content and constraints conflict? Which external target types are permitted?
- Is Artifact Revision a logical state, a physical state or a mapped combination? Which physical changes warrant one?
- What constitutes one physical representation versus components/attachments? Must every technical manifestation mediate through a logical Artifact?
- What explicit governance action establishes acceptance of the aligned model and records supersession/dependencies?

Evidence is insufficient to answer these universally. Hypotheses such as “ADR is a document Artifact of a Decision” are useful analytical separations but do not decide the final type taxonomy or all mappings.

## 21. Conclusion

The current ontology partially distinguishes engineering semantics from documentation and technical serialization. Accepted principles provide a compatible foundation; Draft RFC-0010 and Proposed RFC-0015/0030 contain explicit three-level support. The concrete concept definitions and their cross-model identity, typing, endpoint and evolution contracts remain incomplete or inconsistent.

The analysis supports clarification of the three levels and preservation of logical identity independent of locators. It does not establish disjointness, inheritance, exact cardinalities, a UUID policy, mandatory physical graph objects or a final version model. All 23 current concepts have been examined; no normative definition, schema, tool, ADR, RFC or task artifact has been modified by this analysis.

## 22. Recommended Next Analysis Steps

1. Human-review this original Draft analysis and verify the evidence/status classifications; record corrections as separate commits.
2. Analyze universal Entity scope and Artifact overlap before deriving a taxonomy or narrowing cardinalities.
3. Trace representative Requirement, Decision/ADR, compound RFC, Note and Review/Finding cases through identity/type/owner models, preserving unresolved mappings.
4. Analyze Collection/View definition, membership/evaluation and output identities against version/baseline requirements.
5. Resolve source authority for predicate prose/constraints and distinguish catalogue reconciliation from endpoint redesign.
6. Analyze revision granularity and provenance bindings using concrete semantic versus format-only changes; do not infer historical versions from arbitrary commits.
7. Only after governed semantic decisions, evaluate serialization and tooling conformance in a separate implementation task.

These are proposed analysis steps, not an imposed ATON workflow or an authorization to alter Foundation definitions.
